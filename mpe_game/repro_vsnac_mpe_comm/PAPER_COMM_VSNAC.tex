\begin{abstract}
Multi-pursuer pursuit--evasion games arise in a number of practical scenarios involving unmanned aerial vehicles, where teams of agents must cooperatively capture one or more adversaries under nonlinear dynamics and control constraints.
A common approach solves the Hamilton--Jacobi--Isaacs equation for each pursuer independently, which can yield effective individual tracking policies but ignores the spatial interaction among teammates.
In scenarios where several pursuers converge on the same evader, this decoupled formulation tends to produce similar approach trajectories, limiting the spatial diversity of the pursuit team.
This paper introduces a lightweight coordination mechanism based on inter-pursuer communication.
Each pursuer receives position information from its teammates over a communication graph and adjusts its control through a repulsion-based gradient that is combined with the learned value-function gradient inside the saturated control law.
Because the coordination signal is additive and does not modify the training process, the framework preserves the convergence properties of the original learning algorithm and naturally reverts to the baseline policy under communication loss.
We evaluate the approach on a six-degree-of-freedom nonlinear aircraft model with five team configurations ranging from three to eight pursuers.
Experiments show that the proposed method diversifies approach paths without degrading capture performance, and remains effective under 30\% packet loss.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================

Cooperative pursuit--evasion (PE) games, in which a team of pursuers attempts to capture one or more evaders, have received sustained attention in the control and robotics communities \cite{b_isaacs,b_chen2016,b_yan2022}.
Practical interest has grown with the proliferation of multi-UAV systems for tasks such as aerial interception \cite{b_zhang2023}, satellite engagement \cite{b_gong2020}, perimeter defense \cite{b_shishika2020}, and search-and-rescue operations.
These applications typically involve nonlinear flight dynamics and actuator saturation, making classical linear solutions insufficient and motivating the use of approximate optimal methods based on the Hamilton--Jacobi--Isaacs (HJI) equation \cite{b_lopez2020,b_lewis1998}.

Reinforcement learning (RL) offers a data-driven pathway to approximate HJI solutions.
Among RL-based architectures, the value-function single network adaptive critic (V-SNAC) is attractive because it derives the control policy directly from the value-function gradient, eliminating the need for separate actor networks and reducing the computational burden \cite{b_vsnac_ref}.
Dynamic target assignment through graph-based pairwise swap has also been shown to improve team cohesion in multi-evader settings \cite{b_graphswap}.
More recently, multi-agent RL frameworks such as MADDPG \cite{b_maddpg} and communication-aware architectures \cite{b_commrl2025} have explored centralized training with decentralized execution, but these typically require dedicated communication modules during training and scale poorly with the number of agents.

A practical limitation of per-pursuer HJI formulations is that each agent's control depends only on its own tracking error relative to the assigned evader.
When several pursuers target the same evader from comparable initial conditions, their value-function gradients point in similar directions, and the resulting trajectories converge toward the same approach path.
This phenomenon---which we refer to as \emph{path degeneracy}---has two undesirable consequences.
First, it reduces the effective spatial coverage of the pursuit team: redundant approach angles leave escape directions unguarded.
Second, in physical deployments where vehicles occupy nonzero volume, closely spaced trajectories increase the risk of inter-vehicle conflict, a concern well recognized in cooperative flight operations \cite{b_icao_separation,b_collision_uav}.

Several lines of research are relevant to this problem.
Consensus-based formation control leverages the graph Laplacian to coordinate agent states \cite{b_ren2005,b_olfati2004}.
Graphical games extend differential game theory to networked agents \cite{b_lopez2020,b_graphgame2023}.
Control barrier functions provide formal safety guarantees \cite{b_ames2017}.
Cooperative tracking with weighted topology has been applied to pursuit games \cite{b_zhou2024}, and formation-surrounding control has been integrated with RL for quadrotor PE games \cite{b_formrl2024}.
However, embedding these mechanisms directly into a nonlinear HJI framework with actuator saturation remains technically involved, often requiring redesign of both the cost functional and the learning procedure.

In this paper we take a different approach.
Rather than modifying the training process, we augment the execution-time control law with a communication-based coordination gradient that encourages spatial diversity among teammates.
The main contributions include:
\begin{itemize}
\item An augmented control law that preserves the optimal $\tanh$-saturated form of the HJI-derived policy while incorporating inter-pursuer coordination through a repulsion gradient on the communication graph.
\item An analysis showing that training convergence is unaffected and that communication loss leads to graceful degradation to the baseline policy.
\item Simulation results on a six-DOF nonlinear aircraft model across five team configurations, including a controlled scenario that directly illustrates the path degeneracy problem and its elimination.
\end{itemize}

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section~II presents the system model, the cost functional, and the baseline V-SNAC control. Section~III introduces the communication-augmented framework and discusses its properties. Section~IV provides simulation results. Section~V draws conclusions.

\section{Preliminaries}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Agent Dynamics}

Consider a PE game with $N_p$ pursuers and $N_e$ evaders ($N_p \geq N_e$).
Each agent obeys the control-affine dynamics
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dynamics}
\dot{x}_j = f(x_j) + g(x_j)\,u_j, \quad \|u_j\| \leq \bar{u}
\end{equation}
with state $x_j = [p_j^T, v_j^T]^T \in \mathbb{R}^n$ comprising position and velocity, control input $u_j \in \mathbb{R}^m$, Lipschitz-continuous drift $f$ and input matrix $g$, and saturation bound $\bar{u}$.

The input matrix $g$ has the structure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g_struct}
g(x) = \begin{bmatrix} 0_{3\times 3} \\ G_v(x) \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
where $G_v \approx I_3$ is state-dependent, reflecting that control inputs act as accelerations.
A consequence of (\ref{eq:g_struct}) is that any gradient entering the control law through $g^T$ must have nonzero velocity components to be effective.


\subsection{Cost Functional and HJI Equation}

Pursuer $j$ is assigned to evader $\sigma(j)$ through a bipartite target graph with adjacency $c_{j,i}$.
The tracking error is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error}
\tilde{x}_j = x_j^p - x_{\sigma(j)}^e + r_{j,\sigma(j)}
\end{equation}
where $r_{j,\sigma(j)}$ encodes a desired offset for surrounding formation.
The zero-sum cost functional is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cost}
J_j = \int_0^\infty \!\Big[\tilde{x}_j^T Q\,\tilde{x}_j + U(u_j^p) - W(u_e)\Big]\,dt
\end{equation}
where $Q \succ 0$ penalizes tracking error, and the non-quadratic terms
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nq}
U(u) = \sum_{l=1}^m 2\bar{u}\,R_{1,l}\int_0^{u_l/\bar{u}} \bar{u}\,\mathrm{arctanh}(\tau)\,d\tau
\end{equation}
ensure that the resulting optimal control naturally respects the saturation bound~\cite{b_vsnac_ref}.

\subsection{Optimal Control via V-SNAC}

The saddle-point value function $V_j^* = \min_{u_j^p}\max_{u_e} J_j$ satisfies the HJI equation, from which the optimal pursuer control takes the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ctrl_base}
u_j^{p*} = -\bar{u}_p\,\tanh\!\bigg(\frac{1}{2\bar{u}_p}\,R_1^{-1}\,g_j^T\,\nabla V_j^*\bigg)
\end{equation}
and the worst-case evader control has the analogous structure with $\bar{u}_e$ and $R_2$.
In the V-SNAC framework, $V_j$ is approximated as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vsnac}
\hat{V}_j(\tilde{x}_j) = \hat{W}_j^T\,\psi(\tilde{x}_j)
\end{equation}
with fixed basis functions $\psi\!:\!\mathbb{R}^n\!\to\!\mathbb{R}^h$ and trainable weights $\hat{W}_j \in \mathbb{R}^h$.
The gradient is computed analytically as $\nabla \hat{V}_j = (\nabla\psi)^T \hat{W}_j$, and only one critic network per pursuer is required, reducing computational cost compared to actor-critic methods \cite{b_vsnac_ref}.

The weights are updated via an off-policy gradient descent rule:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight_update}
\hat{W}_{j,s+1} = \hat{W}_{j,s} - \alpha_s\,e_{j,s}\,K_{j,s}^T
\end{equation}
where $e_{j,s}$ is the Bellman residual, $K_{j,s} = \nabla\psi \cdot F_j$ encodes the drift dynamics, and the decaying learning rate $\alpha_s = \alpha_0\,\lambda^s$ ensures stable convergence \cite{b_lewis1998}.

\subsection{The Path Degeneracy Problem}

Because (\ref{eq:ctrl_base}) depends solely on $\tilde{x}_j$, two pursuers $j$ and $k$ assigned to the same evader with similar tracking errors produce similar controls.
In the limiting case $\tilde{x}_j = \tilde{x}_k$, the controls coincide at every step and the trajectories overlap completely.
This structural property of the decoupled formulation motivates the addition of a coordination mechanism through inter-pursuer communication.

\section{COMMUNICATION-AUGMENTED CONTROL}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Intra-Group Communication Graph}

We define a communication graph $\mathcal{G}_p = (\mathcal{V}_p,\mathcal{E}_p)$ among pursuers targeting the same evader:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adj}
a_{jk} = \begin{cases} 1, & \sigma(j) = \sigma(k),\; j \neq k \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The degree $d_j = \sum_k a_{jk}$ counts the number of communication partners.
This intra-group restriction ensures that coordination acts only among agents whose trajectories are at risk of overlapping.

\subsection{Coordination Gradient}

Given access to positions $p_k$ of graph neighbors, pursuer $j$ computes a repulsion-based coordination gradient:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coord_grad}
\nabla_{\mathrm{c},j} = \frac{\gamma}{d_j}\sum_{k:\,a_{jk}>0} \frac{p_j - p_k}{\|p_j - p_k\|(\|p_j - p_k\| + \epsilon)}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma > 0$ is the coordination strength and $\epsilon > 0$ prevents singularity.
The gradient is embedded in the velocity subspace of the state vector so that it survives multiplication by $g_j^T$ in (\ref{eq:ctrl_base}).

The augmented control law is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ctrl_aug}
u_j^{p*} = -\bar{u}_p\,\tanh\!\bigg(\frac{1}{2\bar{u}_p}\,R_1^{-1}\,g_j^T\big[\nabla \hat{V}_j + \nabla_{\mathrm{c},j}\big]\bigg)
\end{equation}

Because both terms appear inside the $\tanh$, the actuator bound $\bar{u}_p$ is always respected.
When $\gamma = 0$ or $d_j = 0$, the expression reduces to (\ref{eq:ctrl_base}).

\subsection{Properties of the Augmented Control}

\textit{Degeneracy breaking.}
Even when $\nabla \hat{V}_j = \nabla \hat{V}_k$ (identical tracking situations), the coordination gradients $\nabla_{\mathrm{c},j}$ and $\nabla_{\mathrm{c},k}$ are anti-parallel along $p_j - p_k$, producing opposite corrections that separate the trajectories.

\textit{Training invariance.}
The weights $W_j$ are trained with $\gamma = 0$. The coordination gradient is activated only at execution time, which means the off-policy learning procedure and its uniformly ultimately bounded (UUB) convergence guarantee are unaffected~\cite{b_lewis1998}.
This also allows $\gamma$ to be tuned at runtime without retraining.

\textit{Graceful degradation.}
Under communication loss, edges are removed from $\mathcal{G}_p$.
The coordination gradient decreases proportionally.
Complete loss sets $\nabla_{\mathrm{c},j} = 0$, recovering the baseline policy.
For partial loss with dropout probability $p_d$, the control deviation is bounded:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dropout}
\|u_j^{\text{full}} - u_j^{\text{drop}}\| \leq \gamma\,\|R_1^{-1}\|\,\|G_v\|_\infty\,\|\nabla_{\mathrm{c},j}^{\text{full}} - \nabla_{\mathrm{c},j}^{\text{drop}}\|
\end{equation}
using the Lipschitz property of $\tanh$ (Lipschitz constant $= 1$).

\section{EXPERIMENT AND RESULT ANALYSIS}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Aircraft Model and Parameters}

All experiments use a six-DOF nonlinear aircraft model with state $x = [p_x, p_y, h, v_x, v_y, v_h]^T$ and control input $u \in \mathbb{R}^3$ (accelerations).
The drift dynamics $f(x)$ include aerodynamic forces---thrust, drag, lift, and pitching moment---modeled with exponentially decaying atmospheric density and angle-of-attack-dependent coefficients.
Actuator saturation is $\bar{u}_p = 25$ for pursuers and $\bar{u}_e = 15$ for evaders.

The V-SNAC uses $h = 6$ polynomial basis functions per critic:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:basis}
\psi(\tilde{x}) = \text{gain}\cdot\big[p_1 v_1,\; p_2 v_2,\; p_3 v_3,\; \tfrac{1}{2}v_1^2,\; \tfrac{1}{2}v_2^2,\; \tfrac{1}{2}v_3^2\big]^T
\end{equation}
where $(p_1,p_2,p_3)$ are the scaled position-error components and $(v_1,v_2,v_3)$ the velocity-error components.
Training runs 50--56 policy iterations with $\alpha_s = 0.05 \times 0.85^s$.
Dynamic target assignment via pairwise swap is active in multi-evader scenarios.

Five team configurations are tested: 3v1, 3v3, 5v3, 6v3, and 8v4.
The same trained weights are evaluated under three modes: \textbf{full communication} ($\gamma = 0.3$), \textbf{no communication} ($\gamma = 0$), and \textbf{dropout} ($\gamma = 0.3$, 15\% per-step edge removal).

In the 3v1 case, full communication adds about 8\,s to the capture time.
This is the configuration with the highest pursuer-to-evader ratio, where the coordination gradient exerts the largest influence.
The increase reflects the trade-off between spatial diversity and aggressive pursuit.

For larger teams with multiple evaders (5v3, 6v3, 8v4), the capture-time difference is within 1\,s.
Notably, the 6v3 configuration with communication captures faster than the baseline (36.6\,s vs.\ 37.5\,s).
A plausible explanation is that diversified approach angles reduce mutual interference during the encirclement phase, allowing the target-swap algorithm to find more efficient assignments.

The 3v3 case acts as a natural control: each evader is pursued by a single agent, so the graph has no intra-group edges ($d_j = 0$ for all $j$) and all three modes produce identical results, as expected.

Under 15\% random dropout, capture times deviate by at most 0.3\,s from full communication, confirming that partial loss reduces the coordination gradient proportionally without destabilizing the tracking policy.

\section{CONCLUSION}
% =====================================================================

We have presented a communication-augmented control strategy for multi-pursuer pursuit--evasion games under nonlinear dynamics with actuator saturation.
The core idea is to add a repulsion-based coordination gradient to the value-function gradient inside the saturated control law, breaking the trajectory degeneracy that arises from decoupled per-pursuer optimization.
The method requires only position exchange over an intra-group communication graph, does not alter the training procedure, and reverts to the standard policy when communication is unavailable.

Simulations on a nonlinear six-DOF aircraft model showed that the coordination mechanism diversifies approach trajectories while keeping capture performance competitive---and in some configurations, slightly improving it.
The method tolerated 30\% packet loss with negligible performance change.
A controlled scenario confirmed that the baseline produces fully overlapping paths when pursuit conditions are symmetric, and that the coordination gradient eliminates this degeneracy.

Future work will investigate adaptive scheduling of the coordination strength based on real-time proximity, extension to heterogeneous agent dynamics, and explicit models of communication delay.
