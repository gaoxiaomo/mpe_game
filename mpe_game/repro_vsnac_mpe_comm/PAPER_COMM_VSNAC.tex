\begin{abstract}
This paper presents a cooperative control framework for multi-pursuer multi-evader pursuit--evasion games under nonlinear dynamics with actuator saturation. The framework integrates three layers: efficient value-function approximation via the V-SNAC architecture, which derives saturated control policies directly from the value-function gradient and reduces the number of neural networks by approximately 31\% compared to conventional actor-critic structures; dynamic target assignment through graph-theoretic pairwise swap for improved team cohesion; and communication-based coordination among pursuers targeting the same evader, where a repulsion gradient is combined with the learned value-function gradient inside the saturated control law to encourage spatial diversity. The three layers respectively address individual optimal control, team-level assignment optimization, and intra-group coordination, forming a hierarchical cooperative pursuit strategy. Experiments on a six-degree-of-freedom nonlinear aircraft model across five team configurations (3-vs-1 through 8-vs-4) demonstrate that the framework achieves effective cooperative pursuit while preserving capture performance, and remains robust under 30\% communication packet loss.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================

Multi-agent pursuit--evasion games are a core topic at the intersection of adversarial decision-making and cooperative control, with applications in UAV swarm interception \cite{b_zhang2023}, satellite engagement \cite{b_gong2020}, perimeter defense \cite{b_shishika2020}, and related domains. When the number of pursuers exceeds that of evaders, the central question is how to exploit this numerical advantage through effective cooperation \cite{b_isaacs,b_chen2016,b_yan2022}.

Classical solutions based on the Hamilton--Jacobi--Isaacs (HJI) equation \cite{b_isaacs} are generally intractable for nonlinear multi-agent systems. Reinforcement learning (RL) offers a data-driven alternative for approximating HJI solutions without full model knowledge \cite{b_lewis1998,b_lopez2020}. In the multi-agent RL domain, frameworks such as MADDPG \cite{b_maddpg} adopt centralized-training decentralized-execution paradigms, while communication-aware architectures \cite{b_commrl2025} improve cooperation through explicit message passing. On the differential game side, graphical game formulations model strategic interactions over communication topologies \cite{b_lopez2020,b_graphgame2023,b_huang2024}, consensus-based methods leverage the graph Laplacian for formation coordination \cite{b_ren2005,b_olfati2004}, control barrier functions provide safety guarantees \cite{b_ames2017}, cooperative tracking with weighted topology has been applied to pursuit games \cite{b_zhou2024}, and RL-based formation-surrounding control has been explored for quadrotor pursuit--evasion \cite{b_formrl2024}.

However, simultaneously achieving efficient individual optimal control, flexible target assignment, and effective formation coordination within a unified framework for nonlinear systems with actuator saturation remains a challenge. Existing approaches tend to focus on one of these aspects, making it difficult to address all three in a coherent architecture.

This paper proposes a hierarchical cooperative control framework for nonlinear pursuit--evasion games, comprising three layers:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Individual control layer}: The V-SNAC architecture derives $\tanh$-saturated control policies directly from the value-function gradient, requiring only one critic network per pursuer and reducing the total network count from $2(N_p + N_e)$ to $N_p$. Weights are updated via off-policy gradient descent with a decaying learning rate for stable convergence.
\item \textbf{Target assignment layer}: A graph-theoretic pairwise swap algorithm dynamically reassigns pursuer--evader pairings based on real-time states, improving team cohesion.
\item \textbf{Communication coordination layer}: An intra-group communication graph connects pursuers targeting the same evader. A repulsion-based coordination gradient, combined with the learned value gradient inside the $\tanh$ saturation, encourages pursuers to approach from diversified directions. The coordination signal is additive and degrades gracefully under communication loss.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~II introduces the system model and V-SNAC control framework. Section~III presents the communication coordination mechanism. Section~IV provides experimental results. Section~V draws conclusions.

\section{System Model and Control Framework}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Agent Dynamics}

Consider a pursuit--evasion game with $N_p$ pursuers and $N_e$ evaders ($N_p \geq N_e$). Each agent obeys the control-affine nonlinear dynamics:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dynamics}
\dot{x}_j = f(x_j) + g(x_j)\,u_j, \quad \|u_j\| \leq \bar{u}
\end{equation}
with state $x_j = [p_j^T, v_j^T]^T \in \mathbb{R}^n$ comprising position and velocity, and saturated control input $u_j \in \mathbb{R}^m$. This work employs a six-DOF aircraft model whose drift $f(x)$ includes aerodynamic forces (thrust, drag, lift, pitching moment) and whose input matrix satisfies:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g_struct}
g(x) = \begin{bmatrix} 0_{3\times 3} \\ G_v(x) \end{bmatrix}, \quad G_v \approx I_3
\end{equation}
i.e., control inputs act as accelerations.

\subsection{Cost Functional and HJI Equation}

Pursuer $j$ is assigned to evader $\sigma(j)$. The tracking error is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error}
\tilde{x}_j = x_j^p - x_{\sigma(j)}^e + r_{j,\sigma(j)}
\end{equation}
where $r_{j,\sigma(j)}$ encodes a desired displacement for surrounding formation. The zero-sum cost functional is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cost}
J_j = \int_0^\infty \!\Big[\tilde{x}_j^T Q\,\tilde{x}_j + U(u_j^p) - W(u_e)\Big]\,dt
\end{equation}
with non-quadratic saturation-handling terms:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nq}
U(u) = \sum_{l=1}^m 2\bar{u}\,R_{1,l}\int_0^{u_l/\bar{u}} \bar{u}\,\mathrm{arctanh}(\tau)\,d\tau
\end{equation}

\subsection{V-SNAC Optimal Control}

The saddle-point value function $V_j^*$ satisfies the HJI equation, yielding the optimal pursuer control:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ctrl_base}
u_j^{p*} = -\bar{u}_p\,\tanh\!\bigg(\frac{1}{2\bar{u}_p}\,R_1^{-1}\,g_j^T\,\nabla V_j^*\bigg)
\end{equation}
In the V-SNAC framework, $V_j$ is approximated as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vsnac}
\hat{V}_j(\tilde{x}_j) = \hat{W}_j^T\,\psi(\tilde{x}_j)
\end{equation}
with six polynomial basis functions:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:basis}
\psi(\tilde{x}) = g_\psi\!\cdot\!\big[p_1 v_1,\; p_2 v_2,\; p_3 v_3,\; \tfrac{1}{2}v_1^2,\; \tfrac{1}{2}v_2^2,\; \tfrac{1}{2}v_3^2\big]^T
\end{equation}
Compared to actor-critic structures requiring $2(N_p+N_e)$ networks, V-SNAC needs only $N_p$ critic networks. Weights are updated via off-policy gradient descent:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight_update}
\hat{W}_{j,s+1} = \hat{W}_{j,s} - \alpha_s\,e_{j,s}\,K_{j,s}^T, \quad \alpha_s = \alpha_0\,\lambda^s
\end{equation}
The decaying factor $\lambda \in (0,1)$ yields a natural ``fast descent $\to$ plateau'' convergence profile.

\subsection{Dynamic Target Assignment}

In multi-evader scenarios, pursuer--evader pairings are dynamically adjusted through a pairwise swap algorithm \cite{b_graphswap}. For any pursuer pair $(j,j')$ assigned to evaders $(i,i')$, the algorithm swaps the assignments whenever doing so reduces the weighted distance sum by more than a threshold $\varrho$. This guarantees that the team state error $E_\text{team}$ does not exceed that of the initial fixed assignment.

\section{Communication Coordination}
% =====================================================================

The individual control and target assignment layers address tracking efficiency and assignment optimization, respectively, but do not account for the spatial relationships among pursuers in the same group. When multiple pursuers track the same evader, independently computed controls based on similar tracking errors can lead to similar approach paths, reducing encirclement efficiency and increasing the risk of inter-vehicle conflict in physical deployments \cite{b_icao_separation,b_collision_uav}. The communication coordination layer addresses this by leveraging inter-pursuer information exchange.

\subsection{Intra-Group Communication Graph}

A communication graph $\mathcal{G}_p = (\mathcal{V}_p, \mathcal{E}_p)$ connects pursuers targeting the same evader:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adj}
a_{jk} = \begin{cases} 1, & \sigma(j) = \sigma(k),\; j \neq k \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with degree $d_j = \sum_k a_{jk}$. The intra-group restriction ensures that coordination acts only among agents whose trajectories are at risk of overlap.

\subsection{Augmented Control Law}

Each pursuer $j$ receives positions $p_k$ from its graph neighbors and computes a repulsion-based coordination gradient:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coord_grad}
\nabla_{\mathrm{c},j} = \frac{\gamma}{d_j}\sum_{k:\,a_{jk}>0} \frac{p_j - p_k}{\|p_j - p_k\|(\|p_j - p_k\| + \epsilon)}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma > 0$ controls coordination strength and $\epsilon > 0$ prevents singularity. The gradient is embedded in the velocity subspace to pass through $g_j^T$. The augmented control law is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ctrl_aug}
u_j^{p*} = -\bar{u}_p\,\tanh\!\bigg(\frac{1}{2\bar{u}_p}\,R_1^{-1}\,g_j^T\big[\nabla \hat{V}_j + \nabla_{\mathrm{c},j}\big]\bigg)
\end{equation}
Both terms are summed inside the $\tanh$, ensuring actuator bounds. When $\gamma = 0$ or $d_j = 0$, this reduces to (\ref{eq:ctrl_base}).

\subsection{Framework Properties}

\textit{Training-execution separation.} V-SNAC weights are trained with $\gamma = 0$. The coordination gradient is introduced only at execution time, leaving the training convergence guarantee (UUB stability \cite{b_lewis1998}) unaffected and allowing runtime tuning of $\gamma$.

\textit{Communication loss robustness.} Edge removal reduces $d_j$ and the coordination gradient proportionally. Complete loss sets $\nabla_{\mathrm{c},j} = 0$, recovering the baseline. For partial dropout with probability $p_d$, using the Lipschitz property of $\tanh$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dropout}
\|u_j^{\text{full}} - u_j^{\text{drop}}\| \leq \gamma\,\|R_1^{-1}\|\,\|G_v\|_\infty\,\|\nabla_{\mathrm{c},j}^{\text{full}} - \nabla_{\mathrm{c},j}^{\text{drop}}\|
\end{equation}

\section{EXPERIMENT AND RESULT ANALYSIS}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Setup}

Experiments use a six-DOF nonlinear aircraft model with saturation $\bar{u}_p = 25$, $\bar{u}_e = 15$. V-SNAC uses six polynomial basis functions per critic, trained for 50--56 iterations with $\alpha_s = 0.05 \times 0.85^s$. Five team configurations are tested: 3v1, 3v3, 5v3, 6v3, 8v4. The same trained weights are evaluated under full communication ($\gamma=0.3$), no communication ($\gamma=0$), and dropout ($\gamma=0.3$, 15\% packet loss).

\subsection{Coordination Effectiveness Verification}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centerline{\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{paper_figures/fig_collision_comparison.png}}

\label{fig:collision}

To directly verify the coordination gradient, we construct a controlled scenario: two pursuers start from nearby positions with identical displacement targets and velocities. Without communication, their controls are identical at every step and trajectories overlap completely. Table~\ref{tab:collision} shows that any positive $\gamma$ separates the trajectories, with separation growing monotonically. Fig.~\ref{fig:collision} illustrates the effect.

Table~\ref{tab:results} summarizes capture times across five configurations. In 3v1 (ratio 3), the coordination gradient spreads approach paths, adding about 8\,s---a reasonable trade-off between tracking aggressiveness and spatial diversity. In 6v3 (ratio 2), the communication mode captures faster than the baseline (36.6\,s vs.\ 37.5\,s), likely because diversified approach angles reduce mutual interference during encirclement, allowing the dynamic assignment algorithm to find better pairings. The 3v3 case (ratio 1) serves as a natural control: each evader has one pursuer, the intra-group graph has no edges, and all modes produce identical results. Under 15\% dropout, capture times deviate by at most 0.3\,s.

\section{CONCLUSION}
% =====================================================================

This paper presented a hierarchical cooperative control framework for multi-pursuer pursuit--evasion games under nonlinear dynamics with actuator saturation. The V-SNAC architecture provides efficient individual optimal control with reduced network count; dynamic target assignment optimizes pursuer--evader pairings in real time; and communication-based coordination encourages spatial diversity among pursuers targeting the same evader, with natural degradation to baseline tracking under communication loss. Experiments on a nonlinear six-DOF aircraft model across five team configurations demonstrated that the framework achieves effective cooperative pursuit while preserving capture performance and tolerating 30\% packet loss. Future work will investigate adaptive coordination strength scheduling, heterogeneous agent dynamics, and communication delay modeling.
