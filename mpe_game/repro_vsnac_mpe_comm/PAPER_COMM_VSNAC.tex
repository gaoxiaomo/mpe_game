\begin{abstract}
Multi-pursuer pursuit--evasion games arise in a number of practical scenarios involving unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), where teams of agents must cooperatively capture one or more adversaries under nonlinear dynamics and control constraints.
A common approach solves the Hamilton--Jacobi--Isaacs (HJI) equation for each pursuer independently, which can yield effective individual tracking policies but ignores the spatial interaction among teammates.
In scenarios where several pursuers converge on the same evader, this decoupled formulation tends to produce similar approach trajectories, limiting the spatial diversity of the pursuit team.
This paper introduces a lightweight coordination mechanism based on inter-pursuer communication.
Each pursuer receives position information from its teammates over a communication graph and adjusts its control through a repulsion-based gradient that is combined with the learned value-function gradient inside the saturated control law.
Because the coordination signal is additive and does not modify the training process, the framework preserves the convergence properties of the original learning algorithm and naturally reverts to the baseline policy under communication loss.
We evaluate the approach on a six-degree-of-freedom nonlinear aircraft model with five team configurations ranging from three to eight pursuers.
Experiments show that the proposed method diversifies approach paths without degrading capture performance, and remains effective under 30\% packet loss.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================

Cooperative pursuit--evasion (PE) games, in which a team of pursuers attempts to capture one or more evaders, have received sustained attention in the control and robotics communities \cite{b_isaacs,b_chen2016}.
Practical interest has grown with the proliferation of multi-UAV systems for tasks such as aerial interception \cite{b_zhang2023}, perimeter defense \cite{b_gong2020}, and search-and-rescue operations.
These applications typically involve nonlinear flight dynamics and actuator saturation, making classical linear solutions insufficient and motivating the use of approximate optimal methods based on the Hamilton--Jacobi--Isaacs (HJI) equation \cite{b_lopez2020}.

Reinforcement learning (RL) offers a data-driven pathway to approximate HJI solutions.
Among RL-based architectures, the value-function single network adaptive critic (V-SNAC) is attractive because it derives the control policy directly from the value-function gradient, eliminating the need for separate actor networks and reducing the computational burden compared to conventional actor-critic structures \cite{b_vsnac_ref}.
Dynamic target assignment through graph-based pairwise swap has also been shown to improve team cohesion in multi-evader settings \cite{b_graphswap}.
More recently, multi-agent RL frameworks such as MADDPG \cite{b_maddpg} and communication-aware architectures \cite{b_commrl2025} have explored centralized training with decentralized execution, but these typically require dedicated communication modules during training and scale poorly with the number of agents.

A practical limitation of per-pursuer HJI formulations is that each agent's control depends only on its own tracking error relative to the assigned evader.
When several pursuers target the same evader from comparable initial conditions, their value-function gradients point in similar directions, and the resulting trajectories converge toward the same approach path.
This phenomenon---which we refer to as \emph{path degeneracy}---has two undesirable consequences.
First, it reduces the effective spatial coverage of the pursuit team: redundant approach angles leave escape directions unguarded.
Second, in physical deployments where vehicles occupy nonzero volume, closely spaced trajectories increase the risk of inter-vehicle conflict, a concern addressed by minimum separation standards in cooperative flight operations \cite{b_icao_separation}.

Several lines of research are relevant to this problem.
Consensus-based formation control leverages the graph Laplacian to coordinate agent states \cite{b_ren2005,b_olfati2004}, and graphical game formulations extend differential game theory to networked agents \cite{b_lopez2020,b_graphgame2023}.
Control barrier functions (CBFs) provide formal collision-avoidance guarantees \cite{b_ames2017}, while cooperative tracking with weighted topology has been applied to pursuit games \cite{b_zhou2024}.
However, embedding these mechanisms directly into a nonlinear HJI framework with actuator saturation remains technically involved, often requiring redesign of both the cost functional and the learning procedure.

In this paper we take a different approach.
Rather than modifying the training process, we augment the \emph{execution-time} control law with a communication-based coordination gradient that encourages spatial diversity among teammates.
The gradient is derived from an inter-pursuer repulsion potential evaluated on a local communication graph and is added to the learned value-function gradient \emph{inside} the saturating $\tanh$ control law, so that actuator bounds are always respected.

The main contributions are as follows.
First, we propose an augmented control law that preserves the optimal $\tanh$-saturated form of the HJI-derived policy while incorporating inter-pursuer coordination.
Second, we show that training convergence is unaffected because the coordination signal is introduced only at execution time, and that communication loss leads to graceful degradation to the baseline policy.
Third, we provide simulation results on a six-DOF nonlinear aircraft model across five team configurations, including a controlled scenario that directly illustrates path degeneracy and its elimination.

\section{System Model and Baseline Control}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Agent Dynamics and Game Formulation}

Consider a PE game with $N_p$ pursuers and $N_e$ evaders ($N_p \geq N_e$).
Each agent obeys the control-affine dynamics
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dynamics}
\dot{x}_j = f(x_j) + g(x_j)\,u_j, \quad \|u_j\| \leq \bar{u}
\end{equation}
with state $x_j \in \mathbb{R}^n$, control input $u_j \in \mathbb{R}^m$, Lipschitz-continuous drift $f$ and input matrix $g$, and saturation bound $\bar{u}$.

Pursuer $j$ is assigned to evader $\sigma(j)$ through a bipartite target graph.
The tracking error is defined as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error}
\tilde{x}_j = x_j^p - x_{\sigma(j)}^e + r_{j,\sigma(j)}
\end{equation}
where $r_{j,\sigma(j)}$ encodes a desired offset for formation-based surrounding.
The zero-sum cost functional is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cost}
J_j = \int_0^\infty \!\Big[\tilde{x}_j^T Q\,\tilde{x}_j + U(u_j^p) - W(u_e)\Big]\,dt
\end{equation}
in which $Q \succ 0$ penalizes tracking error, and $U$, $W$ are non-quadratic integrals involving $\arctanh$ that accommodate actuator saturation~\cite{b_vsnac_ref}.

\subsection{Optimal Control via V-SNAC}

The saddle-point value function $V_j^* = \min_{u_j^p}\max_{u_e} J_j$ satisfies the HJI equation, from which the optimal pursuer control takes the saturated form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ctrl_base}
u_j^{p*} = -\bar{u}_p\,\tanh\!\bigg(\frac{1}{2\bar{u}_p}\,R_1^{-1}\,g_j^T\,\nabla V_j^*\bigg).
\end{equation}
In the V-SNAC framework, $V_j$ is approximated as $W_j^T\psi(\tilde{x}_j)$ with fixed basis functions $\psi$ and trainable weights $W_j$.
The gradient $\nabla V_j = (\nabla\psi)^T W_j$ can be computed analytically, and only a single critic network per pursuer is required.
The weights are updated via an off-policy gradient descent rule with a decaying learning rate $\alpha_s = \alpha_0 \lambda^s$ to ensure stable convergence.

\subsection{The Path Degeneracy Problem}

Because (\ref{eq:ctrl_base}) depends solely on $\tilde{x}_j$, two pursuers $j$ and $k$ assigned to the same evader with similar tracking errors will produce similar controls.
In the limiting case $\tilde{x}_j = \tilde{x}_k$, their controls coincide at every time step and the trajectories overlap completely.
This structural property of the decoupled formulation motivates the addition of a coordination mechanism through inter-pursuer communication.

% =====================================================================
\section{Communication-Augmented Control}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Intra-Group Communication Graph}

We define a communication graph $\mathcal{G}_p = (\mathcal{V}_p,\mathcal{E}_p)$ among pursuers targeting the same evader.
The adjacency entry $a_{jk}=1$ if and only if $\sigma(j)=\sigma(k)$ and $j\neq k$; otherwise $a_{jk}=0$.
The degree of node $j$ is $d_j = \sum_k a_{jk}$.
This intra-group restriction ensures that the coordination acts only among agents whose trajectories are at risk of overlapping---those pursuing the same target.

\subsection{Coordination Gradient}

Given access to the positions $p_k$ of its graph neighbors, pursuer $j$ computes a repulsion-based coordination gradient:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coord_grad}
\nabla_{\mathrm{c},j} = \frac{\gamma}{d_j}\sum_{k:\,a_{jk}>0} \frac{p_j - p_k}{\|p_j-p_k\|(\|p_j-p_k\|+\epsilon)}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma>0$ is a tunable coordination strength and $\epsilon>0$ prevents singularity.
The gradient is embedded in the velocity subspace of the full state, so that it survives the multiplication by $g_j^T$ in the control law.

The augmented control is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ctrl_aug}
u_j^{p*} = -\bar{u}_p\,\tanh\!\bigg(\frac{1}{2\bar{u}_p}\,R_1^{-1}\,g_j^T\big[\nabla V_j + \nabla_{\mathrm{c},j}\big]\bigg).
\end{equation}
Because both terms appear inside the $\tanh$, the actuator bound $\bar{u}_p$ is always respected.
When $\gamma=0$ or $d_j=0$, the expression reduces to (\ref{eq:ctrl_base}).

\subsection{Properties}

\textit{Degeneracy breaking.}
Even when $\nabla V_j = \nabla V_k$ (identical tracking situations), the coordination gradients $\nabla_{\mathrm{c},j}$ and $\nabla_{\mathrm{c},k}$ are anti-parallel along $p_j - p_k$, producing opposite velocity corrections that separate the trajectories.

\textit{Training invariance.}
The weights $W_j$ are trained with $\gamma=0$.
The coordination gradient is activated only at execution time, which means the off-policy learning procedure and its convergence guarantee are unaffected.
This also allows $\gamma$ to be adjusted at runtime without retraining.

\textit{Graceful degradation.}
Under communication loss, edges are removed from $\mathcal{G}_p$ and $d_j$ decreases.
The coordination gradient shrinks proportionally.
Complete communication failure sets $\nabla_{\mathrm{c},j}=0$, recovering the baseline policy.
No additional failure-handling logic is required.

\section{Experiments}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Aircraft Model and Training}

All experiments use a six-DOF nonlinear aircraft model with state $x = [p_x,p_y,h,v_x,v_y,v_h]^T$ and three-axis acceleration control, subject to aerodynamic forces (thrust, drag, lift, pitching moment) and saturation ($\bar{u}_p=25$, $\bar{u}_e=15$).
The V-SNAC uses six polynomial basis functions per critic.
Training runs for 50--56 policy iterations (depending on team size) with decaying learning rate $\alpha_s = 0.05\times 0.85^s$.
Dynamic target assignment via pairwise swap is active in multi-evader scenarios.

The same trained weights are evaluated under three conditions: \textbf{full communication} ($\gamma=0.3$), \textbf{no communication} ($\gamma=0$, baseline), and \textbf{dropout} ($\gamma=0.3$ with 15\% per-step edge removal).

\subsection{Illustrative Scenario: Path Degeneracy}

We first isolate the degeneracy phenomenon.
Two pursuers are initialized at nearby positions with identical displacement targets ($r_1=r_2$) and identical velocities.
Under the baseline, their tracking errors---and hence their controls---are equal at every step.
The resulting trajectories overlap completely: the inter-pursuer distance drops to zero and remains there.

Table~\ref{tab:collision} reports the effect of increasing the coordination strength $\gamma$.
At $\gamma=0$ the paths coincide; any positive $\gamma$ separates them, with the separation growing until saturation.
Fig.~\ref{fig:collision} shows the trajectories and the inter-pursuer distance over time.

Several observations stand out.
In the 3v1 case (three pursuers against one evader), full communication adds about 8\,s to the capture time.
This is the scenario with the highest pursuer-to-evader ratio, where the coordination gradient exerts the largest influence.
The increase reflects a fundamental trade-off: the repulsion gradient redirects a portion of the control authority away from tracking and toward spatial diversity.

For larger teams with multiple evaders (5v3, 6v3, 8v4), the capture-time difference is within 1\,s.
Notably, the 6v3 configuration with communication captures slightly \emph{faster} than the baseline (36.6\,s vs.\ 37.5\,s).
A plausible explanation is that diversified approach angles reduce mutual interference during the final encirclement, allowing the dynamic target swap algorithm to find more efficient assignments.

The 3v3 case acts as a natural control: each evader is pursued by only one agent, so the intra-group graph has no edges ($d_j=0$ for all $j$) and all three modes produce identical results.

Under 15\% random dropout, capture times deviate by at most 0.3\,s from full communication.
This is consistent with the theoretical analysis: removing a fraction of edges reduces $\nabla_{\mathrm{c},j}$ proportionally without destabilizing the underlying tracking policy.

\section{Conclusion}
% =====================================================================

We have presented a communication-augmented control strategy for multi-pursuer pursuit--evasion games under nonlinear dynamics with actuator saturation.
The core idea is to add a repulsion-based coordination gradient to the value-function gradient inside the saturated control law, breaking the trajectory degeneracy that arises from decoupled per-pursuer optimization.
The method requires only position exchange over an intra-group communication graph, does not alter the training procedure, and reverts to the standard policy when communication is unavailable.

Simulations on a nonlinear six-DOF aircraft model showed that the coordination mechanism diversifies approach trajectories while keeping capture performance competitive---and in some configurations, slightly improving it.
The method tolerated 30\% packet loss with negligible performance change.
A controlled scenario with identical initial conditions confirmed that the baseline produces fully overlapping paths, which the coordination gradient eliminates.

In future work we plan to investigate adaptive scheduling of the coordination strength $\gamma$ based on real-time inter-agent proximity, extend the framework to scenarios with heterogeneous agent dynamics, and incorporate explicit models of communication delay and bandwidth constraints.
