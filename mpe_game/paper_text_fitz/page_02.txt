In practical applications, several MPE game processes often
involve inherent nonlinearities [9], [24]. Consequently, solving
the nonlinear Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs (HJI) equations becomes
necessary, for which analytical solutions are not readily avail-
able [25], [26]. To address this issue, various adaptive dy-
namic programming algorithms have been proposed [27], [28],
[29], [30]. In addition, based on the principle of optimality,
reinforcement learning algorithms employing the actor–critic
(AC) architecture have been widely utilized for learning op-
timal policies online in MPE problems [29], [30]. However,
in the context of multiagent systems, each agent is equipped
with its own actor and critic networks [1]. The existence of
multiple networks, while necessary for decentralized decision-
making, inadvertently increases the computational burden [31].
To enhance computational efﬁciency, a single network adaptive
critic (SNAC) structure has been proposed for solving control-
constrained optimal control problems [32], which eliminates the
need for an action network. This structure was later applied
to single-pursuer single-evader games within the framework
of fault-tolerant control [33]. Literature surveys reveal that
existing studies have implemented the SNAC structure in the
context of single-agent PE games and multiagent adversarial
problems [33], [34]. However, no studies have been found to
explore the potential application of this approach within MPE
systems. Therefore, one of the objectives of this article is to
explore the potential of this structure in the MPE system.
Another aspect worth mentioning is that, in engineering ap-
plications, the output amplitudes of actuators often exhibit satu-
rated nonlinearities due to physical limitations [35]. Therefore,
many studies also take into account the design of controllers con-
sidering the actuator saturation phenomenon [36], [37]. Ignoring
the actuator saturation phenomenon not only increases the risk
of performance degradation and instability in controlled systems
butalsoresultsinapartialdetachmentofresearchfrompractical-
ity. Furthermore, the stability and reliability of the systems are of
utmost importance. Currently, mainstream approximate optimal
control methods construct Lyapunov functions to prove the
uniformly boundedness of nonlinear closed-loop systems. The
stability analysis of multiagent systems based on graph-theoretic
approaches is investigated in [38] and [34].
According to the aforementioned discussion, conventional
MPE games present some interesting challenges in designing
coordinated capture strategies among all pursuers. In this article,
our objective is to design an algorithm that combines reinforce-
ment learning and dynamic target graphs to approximate optimal
control strategies for MPE games under actuator saturation
conditions. The contributions of this article are summarized as
follows.
1) A graph-theoretic approach is designed to represent the
relationships among the agents, and based on this, a dy-
namic target graph algorithm is employed to enhance the
coordination among the agents. Unlike the ﬁxed com-
munication graph in [17], [18], and [39], the weighted
adjacency matrix of our graph is entirely dynamic, further
improving the team cohesion among pursuers compared
to [26].
2) In contrast to existing results [12], [26], which solved
MPE games under linear systems, our work considers the
nonlinear dynamic of the system and actuator constraints.
Moreover, unlike [33], our method can obtain solutions
for various scenarios with different numbers of pursuers
and evaders by solving the HJI equation, which implies
that our algorithm faces a more complex environment.
3) In the proposed method, we utilize the value-based sin-
gle network adaptive critic (V-SNAC) network structure,
which reduces the computational burden compared to
the AC network structure [39], [40]. We validate the
effectiveness of our algorithm in two typical scenar-
ios: multiple-pursuer-single-evader and multiple-pursuer-
multiple-evader. In addition, a comparison of the com-
putational burden with the AC structure [39] has been
implemented, and the results indicate that the V-SNAC
structure can reduce computational time by approximately
31%.
The rest of this article is organized as follows. Section II
provides the preliminaries of the MPE problem, including the
graph model and state equations. The solution strategies for the
MPE game are proposed in Section III. The simulations and
experiments for the two scenarios are presented in Section IV.
Finally, Section V concludes this article.
